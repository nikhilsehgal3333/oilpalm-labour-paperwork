Methodology

Results and note · Note as PDF · Code on GitHub · Coding log

An independent desk study by Nikhil Sehgal on public documents only. It is not an output of the University of Bonn, the PALMWORK project or any company named.

This page shows how the study went from six public documents to its findings. Every count on this page is read from the result files in the repository when the site is built.

The question

How do the policy documents of India's oil palm mission (NMEO-OP) and the sustainability disclosures of the processors expanding into North-East India represent labour (on plantations, in mills and in social reproduction) and its link to the environment? What do they leave out that only fieldwork with workers can show?

Each step in detail

1Collect and fingerprint the documents

What goes in. A list of public documents, each with its owner, type, period and web address.

What happens. Each file is downloaded, or saved by hand from a browser where a government server refuses scripted downloads. Its SHA-256 fingerprint, size and page count go into a lock file, and the text is extracted page by page. A later run stops if any file no longer matches its fingerprint.

What comes out. The page text of each document, kept locally, and the lock file, which is published. The documents themselves stay with their owners.

In numbers. 6 of 7 listed documents obtained. The oil palm mission's operational guidelines could not be downloaded and are named as a limit.

Example from this study. The Lok Sabha answer and the oilseeds-mission guidelines were refused to scripts from outside India. The author saved them by hand, and their fingerprints were recorded like every other file.

Files: data/corpus.csv · data/corpus_lock.csv · src/oilpalm_paperwork/corpus.py

2Fix the codebook before coding

What goes in. The four literatures named in the PALMWORK project: labour regime analysis, feminist labour geography, environmental labour studies and political ecology.

What happens. Ten codes are defined, each with a description and a keyword list, and three levels per document and code: explicit for oil palm, generic (company-wide or scheme-wide), absent. The codebook was refined on two documents before any coding and changed once afterwards, with the reason logged.

What comes out. codebook.yaml, version 2.

In numbers. Ten codes: growers, hired workers, pay and price, recruitment and control, health and safety, social reproduction, voice, land and tenure, environment of work, standards.

Example from this study. After the first round, the author split "who works" into growers and hired workers, because labour regime analysis separates growers on their own land from wage workers. Earlier reviews were carried over by matching passages.

Files: codebook.yaml · DEVIATIONS.md

3Find candidate passages

What goes in. The page text of every document and the keyword lists of the codebook.

What happens. For each code, a keyword search marks a window of text around every match and merges overlapping windows. Each candidate is flagged when it sits near an oil palm or North-East word (palm, mill, fresh fruit bunch, Assam, Mizoram and others). Keywords only find candidates; they decide nothing.

What comes out. A count of candidates per document and code (published) and the candidate passages (kept locally, because they are the owners' text).

In numbers. 567 candidate passages across 6 documents and 10 codes.

Example from this study. In the Godrej Agrovet BRSR the code for hired workers has 38 candidates, and 0 of them sit near an oil palm or North-East word: an early sign of the main finding.

Files: results/candidate_counts.csv · src/oilpalm_paperwork/kwic.py

4Code the passages in three layers

What goes in. The strongest candidates for each document and code, plus one row for every empty cell.

What happens. A keyword rule proposes a level (explicit when the passage is near an oil palm word, otherwise generic). A first review reads every passage and corrects the proposal, marks passages that match a keyword in another sense as not relevant, and notes when a passage also counts for a second code. The author then checks the levels.

What comes out. A coding log with all three layers for every row, and short excerpts with page numbers.

In numbers. 137 rows; 120 passages coded; 41 rule proposals changed by the first review; 32 passages not relevant; 1 passage added by hand; 88 passages speak to their code.

Example from this study. "Union Cabinet" matched the keyword for unions and was marked not relevant. The passage on intermediaries in Goalpara, Assam, where growers "reported receiving as less as Rs 5 per kg", was proposed as generic by the rule and corrected to explicit for oil palm.

Files: results/coding_log.csv · results/coded_passages.csv · src/oilpalm_paperwork/coding.py

5Check what decides each result

What goes in. The coded passages and the author's checks.

What happens. Each cell of the result (one document and one code) takes the level of its strongest relevant passage, the deciding passage. The author checked the deciding passage of every non-empty cell, in two rounds.

What comes out. The presence matrix: one level per document and code, with the pages and rows that decide it.

In numbers. 60 cells: 19 explicit, 17 generic, 24 absent. 36 rows checked (20 at the first checkpoint, 16 at the second); 36 of 36 deciding passages checked; 0 levels changed by the author.

Example from this study. The Godrej Agrovet cell for standards is explicit, decided by the passage on PDF page 18 that reports the company's verification under the Indian Palm Oil Sustainability Framework.

Files: results/presence_matrix.csv

6Report from the files, not by hand

What goes in. The presence matrix, the coded passages and the candidate counts.

What happens. A script writes the heatmap, a numbers file and the results block of the README and the note; the note's prose cites documents by page. Tests check that every number in the results block comes from the numbers file, that the published text is clean, and that a second run gives byte-identical results.

What comes out. The figure, the numbers file, the note (Markdown and PDF) and this site.

In numbers. In both BRSRs, 12 of 12 hired-labour cells are generic. Growers appear in an oil palm context in 5 of 6 documents, hired workers in 1.

Example from this study. The results block of the note and of the README is identical, and a test fails if either is edited by hand.

Files: results/manifest.json · src/oilpalm_paperwork/report.py · NOTE.md · tests/test_integrity.py

Levels. explicit: the document says something about the code for oil palm work, workers, growers or places. generic: it addresses the code company-wide or scheme-wide, without tying it to oil palm. absent: it does not address the code. On the coding sheet a passage can also be not relevant when a keyword matched in another sense (for example "Union Cabinet" for the code on trade unions).

Limits

  • Documents show what organisations choose to say. Absence in a document is not absence on the ground.
  • BRSR covers a company's own workforce, so it cannot be expected to show workers employed by growers or contractors.
  • The NMEO-OP operational guidelines are missing.
  • One coder: the author checked every deciding passage, but no second coder assessed reliability.
  • Six documents, in English, from one financial year for the companies; keyword search can miss passages that use other words.
